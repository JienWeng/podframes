# Respond to the person, not just the subject

Original fictional examples for style only. They are not reporting or factual sources.

## Avoid

HOST_A: What are the key benefits of preparing for a podcast?
HOST_B: Preparation is essential because it fosters clarity, engagement, and meaningful insights.
HOST_A: Absolutely. That is a great point.

## Better

HOST_A: I wrote an opening, read it aloud, and deleted the first half.
HOST_B: Was that the part telling people what they were about to hear?
HOST_A: All of it. We hadn't actually said anything yet.
HOST_B: [warm, understated] Efficient. We can waste their time later.
HOST_A: Let's at least make them want to stay first.
HOST_B: Start with what happened. I'll ask when I get lost.

## What to learn

One speaker catches a specific detail and reacts. Both contribute, but not every reply introduces a new fact. Uneven turns, a small concession, and a practical ending feel more conversational than a question-answer-summary template. Do not invent personal experiences for the hosts in a real episode. Adapt the interaction to the selected language without copying these jokes or making every exchange witty.
